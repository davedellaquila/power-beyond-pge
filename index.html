Power beyond PG&E: solar + battery, priced honestly for 2026

You asked for alternatives to PG&E and a serious look at solar + battery for the Fiddletown house — well pump, hot tub, HVAC, Starlink, tree shade, and multi-day PSPS shutoffs included. The short version: there is no other wires utility to switch to, the 30% federal credit for buying a system is gone, and the battery is now the whole economics — but a well-designed system still pencils, and one property-tax deadline is still alive if you move in 2026.

Illustration — a ground-mount array in a clearing, one of the two siting options this research compares for a tree-shaded property. Not a depiction of the actual house.

The verdict in one paragraph

PG&E stays your delivery utility — no Community Choice Aggregator serves Amador County in October 2026, and none is coming in the October 2027 expansion either. What you can change is how much PG&E power you buy at 4–9 p.m. and whether the house stays up in a shutoff. For this house, the resilience-optimal design in the research is solar + 27–40 kWh of battery, optionally plus a propane standby generator — indicatively $50,000–$70,000 for a 10–12 kW rooftop build with 27–30 kWh of storage, before incentives that, for a purchase in 2026, are likely $0. Nothing can be sized responsibly until your 12 months of PG&E interval data is in hand.

2 · Net Billing Tariff

Why the battery is the system, not an add-on

The Net Billing Tariff (the successor to NEM 3.0, CPUC Decision 22-12-056, for interconnections on/after April 15, 2023) replaced retail-rate export credit with the Avoided Cost Calculator — a different export value for every hour of every month. It survived legal challenge; the California Supreme Court declined a final appeal in June 2026.

~3–8¢

What a kWh exported at noon earns, when the grid is saturated with solar

→
55.2¢

What that same kWh used yourself at 6 p.m. avoids buying (E-ELEC summer peak)

→
7–15×

Self-consumption is worth roughly this multiple of export at the hours that matter

What this changes in the design

  • Size the array to daytime load + battery charging, not to maximum export.
  • Store midday surplus and discharge through the 4–9 p.m. window.
  • Shift discretionary loads — well pumping where the pressure tank allows, hot-tub heating, laundry, any future EV — into solar hours.
  • A small number of late-summer evening hours (roughly 5–7 p.m., Aug–Oct) spike to $1–$4/kWh — hours solar alone can't serve, but a charged battery can.

Billing mechanics that are easy to miss

  • ACC export credits split into Supply and Transmission & Delivery components — each can only offset charges of the same type.
  • Credits bank monthly by type across an annual true-up.
  • Cash payment (Net Surplus Compensation) only if the home is a net exporter over the full year — a correctly sized system usually isn't, so leftover credits roll forward.
  • New solar customers are placed on E-ELEC — the CPUC requires it for the Net Billing Tariff.

Source: Energy Toolbase — PG&E NBT charge-type laning; CPUC adoption via Assembly analysis of AB 942.

Solar-only fails this house's brief

Under net billing, a solar-only system exports at cents, imports evenings at up to 55¢, and provides zero outage power — grid-tied inverters shut down when the grid does. It's the cheapest entry and the wrong product for a PSPS home with a well. That's why it isn't in the comparison table below.

4 · Ownership models compared

Five ways to do it — one reference envelope

Reference envelope: 8–14 kW solar, 20–40 kWh battery, rural foothill home. Building blocks Published: California rooftop solar averaged $2.52/W in EnergySage's October 2026 marketplace data (10 kW ≈ $25,165); other 2026 guides put CA rooftop at $2.50–$3.50/W; ground-mount adds +$1.00–$1.50/W; installed battery storage runs roughly $700–$1,000 per usable kWh standalone, less per kWh bundled. Combined totals are Estimate — arithmetic from those blocks, not quotes. In every grid-tied case the ~$24/mo Base Services Charge, nonbypassable charges, and any true-up remain payable — no option produces a literal $0 PG&E bill.

(a) Purchased solar + battery — cash / loan

$40,000–$90,000 gross · rooftop

Estimate Mid-build 10–12 kW + 27–30 kWh ≈ $50,000–$70,000. Ground-mount +$8,000–$21,000; panel upgrade / trenching extra. Federal credit $0; SGIP $0 unless RSSE-eligible. Loan adds interest over 10–25 yrs.

Bill offset Estimate: ~70–90% of energy charges if sized to load and the battery dispatches into 4–9 p.m. Simple payback ~8–12 yrs at PG&E rates per one published 2026 guide for an 8 kW + ~13 kWh build; larger resilience batteries lengthen bill-only payback.

Outage: whole-home while the battery lasts; solar recharges daily. 27 kWh ≈ 2–3 days of disciplined essentials (well, fridge, Starlink, lights, limited HVAC) Estimate; under a day if the hot tub / full HVAC run freely.

  • You own it — all savings and future incentives are yours
  • No escalator, no transfer friction on sale
  • Property-tax exclusion if completed in 2026
  • Largest upfront capital — and 2026 is the most expensive purchase year in a decade, with no 30% credit
  • Maintenance and inverter replacement (~yr 12–15) are yours
  • Payback is sensitive to your actual evening load

(b) Solar lease — provider owns it

$0 down · monthly payment

Solar-only CA marketplace average ~$142/mo (range $74–$269 for ~8.4 kW) Published, EnergySage Oct 2026. Solar + 20–40 kWh battery will price materially higher — $200–$350/mo Estimate. Escalator 0–3%/yr Published range.

Bill offset: the payment replaces most of the energy charge; net saving depends entirely on payment + escalator vs your bill — Estimate modest day-one saving, eroding if the escalator exceeds ~2%.

Outage: only if the lease includes a battery with backup enabled — many solar leases historically did not. Confirm backup (not just bill-credit) operation in writing.

  • Only route that still monetizes the federal 30% (§48E to the provider)
  • Provider owns maintenance / insurance; predictable payment
  • Prepaid variant can mimic ownership economics
  • You own nothing for 20–25 years
  • Escalator compounds — 2.9% ≈ +33% by year 10
  • Transfer / buyout friction when selling; provider must still be performing in year 15; §48E pass-through is partial and opaque — demand the cash-price comparison

(c) PPA — provider owns it, you buy its kWh

$0.17–$0.32 /kWh starting · shopping band

Published ranges $0.12–$0.31/kWh (EnergySage, 25-yr typical), $0.17–$0.27 (one CA provider guide, May 2026), $0.25–$0.32 (NRG, Aug 2026). Escalator 0–2.99%/yr.

Bill offset: you still pay PG&E for all non-solar hours at E-ELEC prices plus fixed charges. PPA kWh are cheap only if rate + escalator stays below PG&E's blended price — Estimate similar to a lease; best case is a 0%-escalator PPA.

Outage: same caveat as a lease — backup exists only if a battery is in the PPA and configured for it.

  • No maintenance; pay only for production
  • §48E pass-through; performance risk sits with the provider
  • 20–25-year contract on your roof; production guarantees vary
  • Per-kWh rate can apply to all production including low-value midday exports in some structures — read how export credits are assigned
  • Home-sale transfer

(d) Battery-only + stay on PG&E

$24,000–$30,000 · 27 kWh installed

Published range for 2× Powerwall 3 class; ~40 kWh ≈ $35,000–$45,000 Estimate. No federal credit; SGIP $0 (general market closed).

Bill offset: small. The E-ELEC arbitrage spread is ~22¢ in summer (55.2¢ peak vs 33.4¢ off-peak) but only ~4¢ in winter (32.1¢ vs 28.5¢) — Estimate a few hundred dollars a year for most homes. The purchase is resilience, not savings.

Outage: excellent for hours to ~2 days of essentials, then dead until the grid returns — no recharge source in a multi-day PSPS. Can time-shift well-pump / hot-tub loads.

  • Lowest complexity; no array, no NBT export math
  • Pairs later with solar — choose a solar-ready platform
  • Worst dollars-per-benefit if bought for bill savings
  • Still pays full PG&E energy prices; battery wears cycling for small arbitrage

The resilience-optimal design for this house

(e) Hybrid — solar + battery + propane standby generator

Cost: option (a) plus the generator — 22 kW Generac-class $10,000–$16,000 installed, 24–26 kW $12,000–$18,000+ Published. Propane tank upsizing, if needed, is extra and unquoted in the research. The generator adds no bill savings — but it may allow a smaller battery than a battery-only multi-day design, partially offsetting its cost Estimate.

Outage: best available. The battery carries the house silently and handles solar self-consumption daily; the generator auto-starts for multi-day PSPS, carries HVAC + well + hot tub together, and recharges the battery / lets solar keep working. Runtime = propane supply — days to weeks with a large tank Estimate, tank-dependent.

  • Only option covering all three goals: bill, evening peak, multi-day outage
  • On propane the generator keeps its full nameplate kW (a “22 kW” unit is ~19.5 kW on natural gas, 22 kW on LP) — no natural-gas service is, for once, an advantage
  • FranklinWH and Generac integrate a generator natively
  • The wood fireplace further stretches fuel in winter outages
  • Highest capital and maintenance — annual generator service, exercise cycles, significant propane burn at load (get gal/hr at 50% load in writing)
  • Most complex permitting: electrical + gas + setbacks + noise; three systems to maintain

The 2026 head-to-head that matters if you have cash

Cash purchase vs prepaid lease/PPA. Multiple 2026 sources report prepaid structures priced below the cash price of the same system because the §48E credit is priced in upfront (one published example: ~$16,000–$18,000 prepaid for a 7.2 kW system vs a higher cash price), with ownership typically transferring after ~5 years. Get both quoted like-for-like — and have a lawyer/CPA read the transfer, warranty, and buyout terms. Whatever you quote, force every offer onto one page: cash price, payment/rate, escalator, production guarantee, backup capability (which loads, what kW), transfer/buyout schedule, and which entity claims §48E on what placed-in-service deadline.

Shaded site?

Enphase (microinverters) or SolarEdge (optimizers) lead on the solar side — every panel works independently of a shaded neighbour.

Multi-day PSPS + generator plan?

FranklinWH or Generac lead on integration — both manage a standby generator natively.

Maximum starting power, simplest pairing?

Tesla Powerwall 3 — 11.5 kW from a single unit, solar inverter built in.

Generators, in brief

Generac Guardian-class air-cooled standby units dominate this segment. Equipment with 200A automatic transfer switch: ~$5,500–$7,400 (22 kW) Published; fully installed $10,000–$16,000 (22 kW) and $12,000–$18,000+ (24–26 kW); Generac's own guidance puts a professionally installed home standby system at ~$8,000–$16,000 including the generator. 8 · Site & practical factors

Foothills realities: shade, fire, snow, permits, timelines

Roof vs ground-mount — shade decides

Rooftop is cheaper by $1.00–$1.50/W Published and stays inside the defensible-space envelope — but tree cover is the controlling fact here. Partial shade on a conventional string drags down the whole string; winter sun angles lengthen tree shadows (deciduous oaks help by leaf-drop; pines don't); roof age, pitch, and fire-code access pathways shrink usable area. Mitigations that work: module-level electronics (Enphase IQ8 microinverters or SolarEdge DC optimizers) plus a proper shade survey.

Ground-mount buys optimal tilt/azimuth in a clearing you choose, no roof-life coupling, easier cleaning and snow shedding, and expansion room. It costs the $/W premium, trenching/conduit to the house (distance-sensitive — get it line-itemed), possible grading, setbacks from property lines / septic / well / propane, and vegetation management under and around the array so it doesn't become a fire pathway — keep the pad gravel/noncombustible and surrounding fuel cleared.

Either way, demand a month-by-month shade/production model (drone + Solar Pathfinder / Solmetric-class survey or equivalent), not a single annual kWh number. Winter production is what charges the battery in PSPS season's shoulder months — a design that looks fine annually can be tree-crippled in December–January.

Your loads, sized honestly

  • Well pump (½ HP class): ~750–1,000 W running, 2,000–3,000 W starting surge. It's the surge, not the ~0.5–1 kWh/day, that sizes the inverter.
  • Starlink (standard kit): ~60 W, ~1.4 kWh/day at 24 h — trivial to run, and the reason the battery keeps the home online through a PSPS.
  • Hot tub — the swing load: get the heater nameplate kW off the unit. Heating a tub through an outage can consume a large fraction of a 27 kWh battery in an evening, so most designs put it on a managed/shed circuit or a “generator-only” rule.
  • HVAC: check compressor starting amps (LRA on the nameplate) against the battery's surge rating; a soft-start kit is a cheap, routine foothill fix.
  • Rule of thumb Estimate: disciplined essentials ≈ 8–12 kWh/day; whole-home unmanaged ≈ 25–40+ kWh/day. That gap is the entire sizing argument for 27–40 kWh + load management.
  • Wood fireplace: a genuine resilience asset — in a winter outage it carries space heat with zero electric load except a small blower (typically a few hundred watts if fitted Estimate), materially extending battery runtime. A winter PSPS is easier on this home than a summer one, when HVAC is electric.

Snow & wildfire

Fiddletown sits at roughly 1,700 ft — snow is occasional rather than alpine, but it happens, and racking must be engineered to the site's design snow/wind loads. Amador County Building states the 2025 California Building Codes apply to all submittals on or after January 1, 2026. Ask the county for the parcel's ground snow load rather than accepting a valley-floor default; steeper ground-mount tilt sheds snow faster. Batteries/inverters belong on a noncombustible wall/pad with code clearances; the generator needs its manufacturer/CARB clearances; propane setbacks interact with array and trench placement. Confirm Amador Fire Protection District expectations early — in a Tier 2/3 district, fire review is not a formality.

Permitting — Amador County

Fiddletown is unincorporated, so the authority is the Amador County Building Department (Jackson): online permitting via its eProcess360 portal (emailed submittals no longer accepted), inspections via hotline. A solar + battery + generator project is typically building + electrical permits, the generator adding gas/mechanical and fire review, a ground-mount adding structural and possibly grading review.

Could not verify: a solar-specific expedited checklist or SolarAPP+ instant-permit offering on the county site. State SB 379 automated-permitting mandates phased in for larger jurisdictions; a small county may still run manual plan review — budget days-to-weeks, and have the installer confirm the current process and fees in writing.

Source: Amador County Building Department

Interconnection — PG&E Rule 21

A separate approval from the county permit, via PG&E's Your Projects portal, ending in Permission to Operate (PTO) — the system may not legally export before PTO. Tariff clocks Published: completeness ~10 business days; Initial Review ~15 business days after validation; Supplemental Review (only if screens fail) ~+20 business days; PTO typically 5–10 business days after complete final electrical clearance, stated maximum ~30 business days. Clean residential jobs are commonly described at 2–6 weeks for the interconnection leg and 6–12 weeks signed-contract-to-PTO overall in 2026 installer guides Published estimates.

File interconnection in parallel with county permitting, not after installation. And note: “installation completed” (property-tax exclusion) and “PTO granted” are different events — ask both the installer and the Assessor which date controls the January 1, 2027 exclusion.

Sources: GreenLancer — PG&E Rule 21 · SunDocket — PG&E (compiled from tariff)

Check the two eligibility wildcards (free, before quotes)(i) RSSE/SGIP income qualification — any chance the household is ≤80% AMI or otherwise RSSE-eligible, get on the waitlist and learn its pre-installation reservation rules; (ii) CARE/FERA/Medical Baseline eligibility. Also verify the PG&E portable backup rebate's current funding if a portable unit interests you.
  3. Decide the completion-year question with the AssessorCall the Amador County Assessor: confirm a system completed in 2026 keeps the §73 exclusion until ownership change, what “completed” means for their roll (final inspection? PTO?), and how a 2027 system would be assessed. This determines whether a 2026 deadline is worth paying a premium for.
  4. Get the shade answer before choosing roof vs groundRequire a site shade survey with monthly production modelling for both a rooftop and a ground-mount design. The cheaper mount is the one with the better winter production per dollar, not the lower $/W.
  5. Solicit three like-for-like quotes on one specification sheetE.g. “10–12 kW, 27–30 kWh, whole-home backup incl. well pump + HVAC (soft start if needed), hot tub on managed circuit, generator-ready” — each quoted three ways: (i) cash purchase, (ii) prepaid lease/PPA with ownership-transfer terms, (iii) monthly lease/PPA at 0% escalator. Require: cash price, escalator, production guarantee, backup loads/kW, §48E safe-harbor status and placed-in-service deadline (TPO quotes), interconnection filing commitment, PTO timeline.
  6. If resilience is the priority, price the generator with the solar, not afterA FranklinWH or Generac-integrated design — or a generator-ready transfer arrangement — is cheaper planned than retrofitted. Get propane consumption (gal/hr at 50% load) and tank-endurance math in the quote.
  7. Do the free PG&E move in parallelRun PG&E's rate-plan comparison on the interval data (E-TOU-C vs E-TOU-D now; E-ELEC once a battery exists). A plan switch costs nothing and starts saving before any hardware arrives.
  8. Do not signAny contract that (a) claims a 30% federal credit on a 2026 purchase, (b) counts a general-market SGIP rebate, (c) has a >0% escalator without a quantified justification, or (d) cannot state its §48E deadline in writing. Verify the installer's CSLB licence (C-10/C-46 as applicable) before any deposit.

10 · Honest gaps

Open questions — and what couldn't be verified

No responsible sizing answer exists without your actual data. These are stated plainly, exactly as the research left them.

Needs your data

  1. Annual and monthly kWh, and the 4–9 p.m. load shape in summer and winter — sets solar kW, battery kWh, and the E-ELEC bill model. Monthly totals alone can't size a battery.
  2. HVAC type/fuel and tonnage; hot-tub heater kW and usage pattern; well-pump HP — sets inverter surge requirements and which loads get managed/shed.
  3. Longest actual outage experienced / target endurance in days — decides battery-only vs hybrid-with-generator, and propane tank sizing. “Multi-day” should be a number (2 days? 5?) first.
  4. Main panel capacity/condition (200A?) and distances for trenching — swing items of $2,000–$10,000+ on any quote Estimate.
  5. Cash available vs financing preference, and expected tenure in the home — decides purchase vs prepaid-lease vs PPA, and how heavily to weigh the property-tax exclusion's “until ownership change” benefit.
  6. Income-program eligibility (CARE/FERA/RSSE) — the only remaining route to a five-figure incentive.
  7. Roof age/condition vs ground-mount clearing location — decides mount type after the shade survey. Plus: current rate plan, baseline territory, propane tank size/ownership (leased tanks restrict who may fill / attach a generator line), and the address's actual PSPS/outage history.

Could not verify in this research

  • A solar-specific permitting checklist, fee schedule, or SolarAPP+ availability for Amador County.
  • The exact current funded status of PG&E's portable Generator & Battery Rebate — sources conflict (open with a Dec 31, 2026 deadline vs reported full/closed in August 2026).
  • PG&E's NBT26 hourly ACC export price file for a 2026 interconnection vintage — the mechanism and ranges are sourced; the parcel-specific 8,760-value file was not pulled. Any serious quote should model from that file.
  • Whether the specific parcel is HFTD Tier 2 or Tier 3 — confirm via the CPUC HFTD map / PG&E wildfire map for the exact address.
  • DAC-SASH disadvantaged-community tract status for the address.
  • Local (Amador / El Dorado foothill) installer depth and lead times for FranklinWH and Generac PWRcell 2 specifically — platform choice may be constrained by who will actually service the system within a reasonable drive.
